# Code of conduct

Flow is made by people in different countries, writing in different
languages, at every level of experience. This page says how we treat each
other while we make it.

## Where it applies

In Flow's repositories (Flow-Terminal, Flow-releases, Flow-SDK and the
others under arcanii): issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions and
commit messages, and anywhere someone speaks for Flow.

## What we do

- Be kind and patient. Assume the other person means well, and ask before
  deciding they don't.
- Talk about the code, the design or the words, never the person who wrote
  them.
- Make room for newcomers. A question asked for the first time is a good
  question, and English needn't be anyone's first language here.
- Say what's wrong plainly and what would make it right. Take the same
  from others.
- Accept a decision you argued against, once it's made, or say why it
  should be made again.

## What we don't

- Insults, slurs, or jokes at anyone's expense, about who they are or where
  they come from.
- Harassment of any kind: following someone around the project, unwanted
  attention, or keeping on after being asked to stop.
- Sexual language or pictures.
- Threats, or encouraging anyone to harm themselves or others.
- Publishing someone's private information (their name, address, email or
  anything else they haven't made public) without their permission.
- Trolling, or derailing a discussion again and again.

## Reporting

Report anything that breaks this code privately, through Flow-Terminal's
**Security** tab: **Report a vulnerability**, with a title that starts
with **Conduct:**. Only the maintainer sees it there. Say what happened,
where (links help most) and who was involved. Your name is never passed
on.

If the report is about the maintainer, report it to GitHub instead, with
**Report content** on the comment or the account.

## What happens

The maintainer reads every report and answers it. Depending on what
happened, that may be a word in private, an edited or hidden comment, a
locked discussion, or someone blocked from Flow's repositories for a time
or for good. GitHub's own [Community
Guidelines](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-community-guidelines)
apply everywhere on GitHub as well.
